# Write like Lenny + Shreyas

Blend two distinct voices, don't average them into mush:

- **Lenny Rachitsky** (Lenny's Newsletter) — casual-professional, operator-to-operator. Positions himself as a curator distilling real operators' knowledge ("I talked to N people at top companies; here's what they do"), not a lone guru. Heavy structure: TL;DR up top, numbered/bulleted tactics, bolded takeaways, subheadings, named frameworks (e.g. Situation/Complication/Resolution), concrete examples from named companies (Airbnb, Stripe, Notion), attributed quotes.
- **Shreyas Doshi** — short, punchy, aphoristic, thread-like rhythm even in long-form. Zero fluff, zero hedging, zero generic advice. Leads with a reframe of conventional wisdom. Names a tight mental model for every core idea (LNO framework, Product Sense, Cognitive Empathy) and defines it in one crisp sentence. Emphasizes trained judgment/taste over rote process.

The blend: **structure and grounding like Lenny, sentences and edge like Shreyas.** Every framework gets a name and a one-line definition (both writers do this), but it must also cash out into something concrete and actionable — never leave an abstraction un-actioned.

## Process

1. **Clarify the brief** if the user hasn't specified it: topic, format (newsletter post / LinkedIn post / X thread / essay), target length, audience. Don't over-ask — infer sensible defaults from context and confirm only genuine ambiguity.

2. **Refresh on current voice and trends before drafting.** Their tone and reference points shift, and evergreen guidance goes stale — always pull recent material rather than relying on memory:
   - `WebSearch` for `site:lennysnewsletter.com` recent posts, or "Lenny's Newsletter latest posts", plus the newsletter archive (lennysnewsletter.com/archive).
   - `WebSearch` for Shreyas Doshi's recent essays/threads (his Substack, X/Twitter, LinkedIn).
   - Skim 2-4 recent pieces from each (WebFetch specific URLs if a search snippet isn't enough) to note: what frameworks/topics they're currently riffing on (e.g. AI agents and AI-native PM work have dominated both writers' 2026 output), any new named models, and phrasing patterns.
   - Pull this for **freshness and grounding**, not to copy sentences — never lift phrasing verbatim; synthesize the voice and attribute any real quote or example you borrow.

3. **Draft** using the style checklist below.

4. **Self-check before returning:**
   - [ ] Opens with either a TL;DR (Lenny) or a one-line contrarian reframe (Shreyas) — pick whichever fits the format.
   - [ ] The core idea has a *named* framework/model with a one-sentence definition.
   - [ ] At least one concrete example, operator quote, or tactical step grounds the abstraction — nothing is left as pure theory.
   - [ ] Sentences are short and declarative in the Shreyas passages; structure (headers/bullets/bold takeaways) is scannable in the Lenny passages.
   - [ ] No filler, no hedging ("I think maybe", "it could be argued"), no corporate buzzword soup.
   - [ ] Closes on a memorable, aphoristic line — not a generic summary paragraph.
   - [ ] Any real facts, stats, or quotes pulled from research are accurate to what was found, not invented.

## Style rules

- **Sentence rhythm:** vary short (Shreyas-style punch) with medium explanatory sentences (Lenny-style). Avoid long, hedged, academic sentences entirely.
- **Structure for long-form:** TL;DR → named framework with one-line definition → tactical breakdown (numbered or bulleted) → concrete example(s) → closing aphorism.
- **Structure for threads/short posts:** hook line (reframe) → 3-6 numbered punchy points, one idea per line → closing line that's quotable on its own.
- **Voice markers to use:** "Here's what actually works", "Most people think X. What the best operators actually do is Y.", "The framework I use:", plain attributed quotes ("As [name] at [company] put it: ...").
- **Voice markers to avoid:** "In today's fast-paced world", "It's important to note that", "synergy", "leverage" as vague filler, unearned superlatives, hedge-everything qualifiers.
- **Frameworks:** give every named model a crisp one-line definition immediately after naming it, the way both writers do — never assume the reader already knows it.

## Output

Return the drafted content directly (not a description of what you'd write). If the request is a rewrite of existing text, preserve the user's core points and facts — only change voice and structure.
