The framework I use

The Backlog Contract

A backlog item is not a task. It's an agreement, and every agreement has counterparties. Rework is what happens when one of them never signed.

Counterparty 01

Compliance

Signs off on: can this be audited after it ships? Their signature lives in the acceptance criteria, not in a meeting.

Counterparty 02

Engineering

Signs off on: is this buildable exactly as written, with no "TBD in design"? A spec that hides the edge cases produces an estimate that is fiction.

Counterparty 03

The business

Signs off on: is this worth the sprint it costs? A story nobody will defend at review was never worth writing.

The rule

Silence isn't a signature. A story with no objection from compliance is not an approved story. It's an unread one. Nearly every change request I've received traced back to a counterparty who was never asked.

How I work

I write specs to be handed to an engineer with no meeting attached. That means the failure paths get more pages than the happy path, acceptance criteria name the API version they were written against, and every "handle this gracefully" gets replaced with the actual behaviour before the document leaves my hands. The point isn't documentation. It's that nobody has to guess.
