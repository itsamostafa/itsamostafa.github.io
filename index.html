Product Philosophy

In a regulated space, the backlog isn't just a list of features — it's a contract with compliance, engineering, and the business all at once. I believe good product work means writing requirements precise enough for audit, clear enough for engineering, and valuable enough for the business — without ever losing sight of the person using the platform.

Product Thinking

How I think as a Product Manager

01

Understand the Business Problem

Getting to the real legal, financial, and user problem before writing a single user story — assumptions stay hypotheses until validated against compliance and operational reality.

02

Map As-Is, Find the Gaps

Walking the current process end to end, then running gap and feasibility analysis to separate what's broken from what's simply undocumented.

03

Design the To-Be & Define the MVP

Turning the target state into BRDs, PRDs, user stories, and acceptance criteria — precise enough for audit, clear enough for engineering, and cut down to what's worth shipping first.

04

Agile Delivery

Owning sprint planning, reviews, and retrospectives — Scrum and Nexus in practice, not just in theory.

05

Stakeholder Alignment

Keeping legal, compliance, delivery, and operations moving together, so delivery meets regulatory and financial workflow standards the first time.

06

UAT, Rollout & Improvement

Seeing features through UAT and rollout, then feeding outcomes back into the backlog — the discipline that helped cut rework by 30%.
