The framework I use

The Backlog Contract

A backlog item is not a task. It's an agreement, and every agreement has counterparties. Rework is what happens when one of them never actually signed.

Counterparty 01

Compliance

Signs off on: can this be audited after it ships? Their signature lives in the acceptance criteria, not in a meeting.

Counterparty 02

Engineering

Signs off on: is this buildable exactly as written, with no "TBD in design"? A spec that hides the edge cases produces an estimate that is fiction.

Counterparty 03

The business

Signs off on: is this worth the sprint it costs? A story nobody will defend at review was never worth writing.

The rule

Silence isn't a signature. A story with no objection from compliance is not an approved story. It's an unread one. Nearly every change request I've received traced back to a counterparty who was never actually asked.

Case Study · Product Manager Take-Home

Why Users Stop Coming Back

Daleel Store's brief called it a retention problem. The first thing I did was argue with the brief.

Result

2 user types, not 1Splitting "churn" into users who were failed and users who simply finished changed the metrics, the initiatives, and what got deferred.

Context

Signups growing, retention collapsing after the first purchase. No access to internal data, so every claim had to be written as a testable hypothesis.

Problem

A churned user and a finished user look identical on a retention chart. Optimising them the same way builds re-engagement machinery for people who were never coming back.

What I did

Named the Finished/Churned Split, separated three diagnoses that all look like falling retention, then built three initiatives and a 90-day plan on top of the distinction — plus an explicit list of what I deferred.

Featured Case Study · Product Spec

The Zero-Friction Amazon Listing Wizard

A 26-page PRD taking a first-time seller from "I have a product" to a live Amazon listing, specified against the Selling Partner API and written to be built without a meeting attached.

Result

≤ 25 keystrokesA measured typing ceiling, 11 steps, 23 specified edge cases, and a Definition of Done written as conditions you can check rather than adjectives.

Context

Listing on Amazon means understanding product-type taxonomies, per-category attribute schemas that differ by marketplace, image specs, and Seller Central itself. First-time sellers have none of that.

Problem

Amazon's requirements change per category, per marketplace, and over time. Any spec that hardcodes them is correct the day it ships and quietly wrong afterwards.

What I did

Opened with Rule Zero — eight system rules that outrank the rest of the document, and a stated tiebreaker. Made validation schema-driven at runtime, used Amazon's own dry-run mode as the source of truth, and listed the out-of-code prerequisites with a named owner each.

Problem
