Cross-Functional Alignment

Three yeses before we build

Most rework isn't a coding problem. It happens when someone who should have weighed in didn't. I get three groups to agree before a story reaches a sprint.

Compliance

Can we stand behind it?

Regulatory needs go into the acceptance criteria up front, so nobody discovers them after launch.

Engineering

Can we build it as written?

Edge cases are written down, not left as "TBD." If the edge cases are missing, the estimate is a guess.

Business

Is it worth the sprint?

If nobody will defend a story at review, it shouldn't be in the backlog.

The rule

No answer isn't a yes. If compliance hasn't objected, they may simply not have read it yet. Most change requests I've seen trace back to someone nobody asked.

About Me

Engineer first. Builder always.

My Journey

I started in Electronics & Communications Engineering, where I led a team building FleetX, a smart fleet management system backed by ITIDA and ASRT. From there I moved into software testing, then into product. Today I turn fintech and investment workflows into features for a live enterprise SaaS platform. Along the way I founded and led CIC's Google Developer Student Club (Top 50 globally, Top 20 MENA at the Google Solution Challenge) and ran programs with IEEE and Google Developer Groups.

How I work

I start with the user's problem. I talk to the people dealing with it and watch where they get stuck. Then I check feasibility early. My engineering and testing background means I spot the edge case or integration that could break a plan before it reaches a sprint. What the team gets from me is short and specific: the problem, what matters, and how we'll know it works. Engineers can start building without another meeting.
